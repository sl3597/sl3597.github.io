I am an incoming **Applied Physical AI Researcher** at **Siemens** (Autonomous Factory team, Digital Industries) in Suzhou, starting in October 2026, where I will work on bringing multimodal foundation models and robot learning into real-world industrial applications. Previously, I was a **High-Performance Computing Engineer** at **Zhuoyu Technology (DJI Automotive)** in Shenzhen, where I built model export, compilation, evaluation, and on-device deployment pipelines for in-vehicle AI models.

I received my M.Eng. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Cornell Tech, Cornell University (Merit Scholarship), where I was a **Research Assistant** in the Computer Systems Laboratory, advised by [**Prof. Mohamed Abdelfattah**](https://www.mohsaied.com/), working on low-bit quantization for LLMs, VLMs, and diffusion models.

Before Cornell, I received my B.Eng. in Electronic and Electrical Engineering with First-Class Honors from University College London (UCL), and then worked as a **Research Assistant** at Tsinghua University under the supervision of [**Prof. Shuwen Deng**](https://www.thu-haslab.org/author/shuwen-deng/), where I conducted research on Compute Express Link (CXL) and memory systems.

## Research Interests

I am interested in **Physical AI** — bringing multimodal foundation models out of the lab and into real-world industrial systems — and in the **ML systems** that make these models fast and reliable enough to run on physical hardware:

- **Embodied & Industrial Physical AI** — Applying multimodal foundation models and robot learning (VLMs, VLAs, world-action models) to industrial automation, and closing the gap between lab benchmarks and real factory scenarios.  
- **Multimodal Foundation Models** — Data preparation, fine-tuning, and evaluation of LLMs, VLMs, and diffusion models, with an eye toward perception-to-action pipelines.  
- **Efficient Inference & Deployment** — Low-bit quantization, custom GPU kernels (Triton), and model compilation for real-time inference on edge, robotic, and in-vehicle platforms.  
- **Hardware–Software Co-design** — Memory-centric systems (CXL, disaggregated memory) and digital hardware design, from RTL to GDSII, to understand efficiency bottlenecks across the full stack.
